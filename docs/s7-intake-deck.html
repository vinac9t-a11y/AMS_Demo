Step 2 · Clarify

It asks before it assumes

  • Four targeted questions — the ones a scrum master would ask, not a checklist.
  • Blanks are honest: "1 of 4 answered — blanks become stated assumptions."
  • Every model call is measured — seconds and tokens in the activity log, taken from the provider, never invented.
Step 3 · Draft plan

Coverage is computed, never claimed

  • Requirement inventory first — then every requirement must be claimed by a story. The mapping is calculated from ids, not taken from the model's word.
  • Assumptions stay visible — carried in amber until answered.
  • DRAFT badge until a human signs. Nothing pretends to be final.
Step 4 · Human review

The reviewer shapes the plan — under the same rules as the AI

  • Reassign, re-estimate, move — directly on the story card.
  • Skills are enforced for humans too: assigning a story to someone whose streams don't cover it is refused, with the reason.
  • Every change is logged and the story is badged human-shaped.
  • Or send it back: written feedback triggers an AI revision — re-checked by the same validator, capped at two rounds.
Step 5 · The gate

No plan ships unsigned

  • A named approver and a sign-off note — recorded with a timestamp.
  • Approval locks the plan. Every edit path afterwards is refused by the server: "approved and locked (by Alan Lands)".
  • Team load stays visible — anyone carrying over half the plan is flagged before sign-off, not after.
Why this can be trusted in front of a client

Governance is the feature

Provenance on everything

Every artifact is badged LIVE AI, REPLAYED AI, or STAGED. A prepared artifact is never presented as a live result — the one failure that loses the room.

Rules live on the server

Roster-only assignment, skill coverage, the estimate scale, story quality bars, coverage checks — enforced in one engine no screen can bypass. The UI's dropdowns are a courtesy; the server is the law.

Decisions leave a trail

Who reassigned, who re-estimated, what feedback triggered a revision, who signed — a review log answers "who changed that?" with a record, not a memory.

Honest coverage over claimed magic

An articulated "here is what AI did, and here is what a human decided" beats a claimed 100% that does not survive the first question.

Where this goes next · design approved 2026-08-06

From a screen to an artifact

  • Artifact plane: every session persisted as files — approval produces plan.json (machine handoff) and plan.md (the signed record). Restart-proof, with session history.
  • Full text control: the reviewer edits story titles, narratives and acceptance criteria inline — with per-field provenance.
  • Dependency-aware planning: a story can never be scheduled before what it depends on.
  • Answer-an-assumption: assumptions become questions the reviewer answers; the plan absorbs the answers.
  • Then downstream: the approved plan feeds build → test → docs → release — the second half of the S7 story.
