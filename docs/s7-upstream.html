Upstream — agree what "done" means.
Before anything gets built.

The upstream loop's only product is a definition of success — not code, not designs: signed, testable acceptance criteria. It runs live today, end to end, on real model calls.

The loop LIVE

The five beats, in order

📄1 · Epic in Paste, upload or drag the business epic into the app.
→
💬2 · AI clarifies Asks only questions that change the plan. Unanswered ones become stated assumptions, visible on the stories they affect.
→
🗂3 · Draft plan Requirements inventory first, then stories with acceptance criteria, estimates, owners and sprints. A DRAFT badge until a human signs.
→
🧑‍💼4 · Human shapes Reassign leads, re-estimate, move stories between sprints — or send the whole draft back with feedback for an AI revision.
→
✍️5 · Sign-off GATE 1 A named approver and a note. The plan locks; the server refuses every later edit.

What leaves the loop

THE CONTRACT — signed acceptance criteria
Written to plan.json (the machine handoff) and plan.md (the signed, readable record). Downstream cannot finish until these criteria are provably met, and nobody can quietly change them after sign-off — a wrong criterion means a new upstream round, on the record.

Why this beats "epic → stories" in one hop

1 · Unknowns become visible
A one-hop breakdown silently guesses at everything the epic leaves open. Here every open question is a stated assumption the room can see.
2 · Coverage is proven, not claimed
Every requirement must be claimed by a story — computed from ids. "Did we miss anything?" is answered by a table, not a feeling.
3 · A human owns the result
Nothing reaches execution unsigned. The plan carries a name, a timestamp and a review trail — that is what makes the automation after it defensible.
