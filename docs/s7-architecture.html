02

Upstream — plan the work

LIVE

The half that runs today, end to end, on real model calls. Two bounded loops, a set of human controls, and a gate that locks. This is where an epic stops being a document and becomes a committed, owned, estimated plan.

A

The shape — two loops, then a lock

B

What the human actually controls

🎛 On every story card
Lead (skill-matched roster only) · estimate (Fibonacci scale) · sprint (dependency-checked). Each change badges the story human-shaped and lands in the log.
↩️ On the whole draft
Written feedback sends the plan back — the AI revises, the validator re-checks, capped at two rounds so revision is a decision, not a slot machine.
✍️ At the gate
A named sign-off with a note. After it: locked. The server refuses every edit, naming the approver — immutability as a rule, not a greyed-out button.
C

Why this beats "epic → stories" in one hop

1 · Unknowns become visible
A one-hop breakdown silently guesses at everything the epic leaves open. Here every unanswered question is carried as a stated assumption on the exact story it affects — the room can see what the plan rests on.
2 · Coverage is proven before commitment
The requirements inventory comes first, and every requirement must be claimed by a story — computed from ids, not asserted. "Did we miss anything?" is answered by a table, not a feeling.
3 · A human owns the result
Nothing reaches execution unsigned. The plan that enters the downstream lane carries a name, a timestamp and a review trail — which is what makes the automation after it defensible.
03

The artifact plane — files, not conversations

DESIGNED

Every meaningful thing the pipeline produces becomes a file at a path you can predict, validated against the contract before anything consumes it. Nothing dies with a process, every handoff is checkable, and any artifact traces back to what caused it.

C

Why files beat chat for the handoff

🔁 Survives everything
Restart, crash, laptop change — the session reloads from disk. A demo interrupted mid-beat resumes instead of restarting.
🔀 Crosses the surface switch
Browser-side planning and IDE-side execution use different tools and different models. Context carries because the handoff is a validated file at a deterministic path — not a conversation the next model never saw.
🔍 Checkable before use
Every consumer validates the file against the contract before running. A stage fed a bad artifact refuses loudly instead of guessing.

06

The governance shape — two loops, one contract

The whole system in one sentence: the first loop argues until a human signs what "done" means · the contract carries that definition across · the second loop builds until "done" is provably met. And in neither loop does anything mark its own work correct.

1

Upstream loop — agree what "done" means

LIVE
▼
THE CONTRACT — signed acceptance criteria in plan.json · upstream ends by producing it · downstream cannot end until it satisfies it · locked after sign-off
▼
2

Downstream loop — build until "done" is provably met

PLANNED · Sprint 2
🔒 Why the contract sits between them
Downstream never reinterprets what "done" means, and upstream cannot quietly change it after sign-off. If the criteria turn out wrong, the fix is a new upstream round, on the record — not a downstream improvisation.
⚖️ Why the same shape twice
Both loops kill the same failure: a generator grading its own homework. The model that wrote the plan doesn't approve it — the validator and a human do. The step that wrote the code doesn't declare it done — the tests and the criteria check do.
🧑 Why the human is a node, not a spectator
Upstream: answers, shapes, signs. Downstream: steers the agent, approves the release. The human is a working part of both cycles — never someone being shown finished results.
